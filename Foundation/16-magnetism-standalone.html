4.1 Magnetism

Meet the physics of magnets: poles, forces, magnetic materials, induced magnetism, field lines and electromagnets — the foundation for everything else in the electricity and magnetism topic.

3Notes

Core
  • Like poles repel (N-N, S-S); unlike poles attract (N-S).
  • Magnetic materials (iron, cobalt, nickel, steel) are attracted by a magnet and can be magnetised. Non-magnetic materials (plastic, aluminium, copper) cannot.
TypeDescriptionExampleUse
Hard (permanent)Hard to magnetise; keeps magnetism wellHard steelPermanent magnets, compasses
Soft (temporary)Easy to magnetise; loses it quicklySoft ironElectromagnet cores

Induced magnetism: a magnet attracts an unmagnetised magnetic material (e.g. a steel pin) by inducing an opposite pole in the near end. Remove the magnet and the effect fades.

A magnetic field is a region where a magnetic pole feels a force, shown with field lines (plot with a compass or iron filings): lines run N→S (direction of force on a N pole there); closer lines = stronger field; lines never cross.

Electromagnet (solenoid): a coil that's magnetic only while current flows. Strengthen it by: more current, more turns of wire, or a soft iron core. Switchable → used in scrapyard cranes, doorbells, motors, relays.

Supplement (Extended only)

Attraction/repulsion happens because two magnets' fields interact and combine; magnets move from stronger field towards weaker field. Line spacing shows relative field strength — compare two points/fields by how close the lines are.

Full explanations and worked examples: Coursebook Chapter 16.

4Key terms

Pronunciation reference: British Received Pronunciation (RP). /ə/ = the soft "uh" sound in an unstressed syllable — this sound doesn't exist in Bahasa Indonesia.

Magnetic polenoun
One of the two ends of a magnet (north or south), where the magnetic force is strongest.
/mæɡˈnetɪk pəʊl/mag-NET-ik pole
🇮🇩kutub magnet
"Like magnetic poles repel each other."
"Kutub magnet yang sama akan tolak-menolak."
Induced magnetismnoun
Magnetism temporarily created in a magnetic material when it is placed near a magnet.
/ɪnˈdjuːst ˈmæɡnɪtɪzəm/in-DYOOST MAG-ni-tiz-uhm
🇮🇩kemagnetan terinduksi
"Induced" sounds like "in-DUCED", not "IN-duced" — stress the second syllable.
"A steel pin shows induced magnetism near a bar magnet."
"Peniti baja menunjukkan kemagnetan terinduksi di dekat magnet batang."
Ferrousnoun
Containing iron.
/ˈferəs/FEH-ruhs
🇮🇩mengandung besi (feros)
"Steel is a ferrous alloy because it contains iron."
"Baja adalah paduan feros karena mengandung besi."
Solenoidnoun
A coil of wire that produces a magnetic field when a current flows through it.
/ˈsəʊlənɔɪd/SOH-luh-noyd
🇮🇩solenoida
"A solenoid acts like a bar magnet when switched on."
"Solenoida bertindak seperti magnet batang ketika dinyalakan."
Hard magnetic materialnoun
A material (like steel) that is difficult to magnetise but keeps its magnetism for a long time.
/hɑːd mæɡˈnetɪk məˈtɪəriəl/hard mag-NET-ik muh-TEER-ee-uhl
🇮🇩bahan magnetik keras
"Steel is a hard magnetic material, used for permanent magnets."
"Baja adalah bahan magnetik keras, digunakan untuk magnet permanen."
Soft magnetic materialnoun
A material (like soft iron) that is easy to magnetise but loses its magnetism quickly.
/sɒft mæɡˈnetɪk məˈtɪəriəl/soft mag-NET-ik muh-TEER-ee-uhl
🇮🇩bahan magnetik lunak
"Soft iron is used as the core of an electromagnet."
"Besi lunak digunakan sebagai inti elektromagnet."

6Theory check

Aim for 100% — if you miss any, press "Try again" and have another go before moving on.

T1 A student lines up two permanent bar magnets with a soft iron nail placed between them, touching both magnets end-to-end. The nail becomes magnetised by induction where it touches each magnet. If the magnet pole touching end X of the nail is a north pole, and the magnet pole touching end Y of the nail is also a north pole, what are the induced poles at locations X and Y (the free ends of the nail, away from the magnets)?

T2 Two bar magnets are stored with metal 'keeper' bars placed across their ends, to help them stay magnetised. The keeper material becomes strongly magnetised when touching the magnets, but loses this magnetism as soon as it is taken away. What is a suitable metal for the magnets, and what is a suitable metal for the keepers?

T3 A student counts how many iron pins an electromagnet picks up with its power supply switched on, then switched off, repeating for cores of different materials. Which result (pins picked up ON, pins picked up OFF) would you expect from a core made of soft iron?

T4 Material 1 magnetises easily and loses its magnetism as soon as it is removed from a magnetic field. Material 2 is difficult to magnetise but retains its magnetism for a long time. Which pairing shows the most suitable use for these two materials?

T5 A student has three metal rods, 1, 2 and 3. She holds end V of rod 1 near each end of rods 2 and 3. End V repels one end of rod 2 but attracts the other end of rod 2, and attracts both ends of rod 3. Which rods must be magnets?

T6 A student has what they believe is a permanent magnet and wants to find out which end is its North pole. What could they do?

9Flashcards

Tap a card to flip it.

RuleLike and unlike poles
Like poles repel (N-N, S-S); unlike poles attract (N-S).
MaterialsHard vs soft magnetic material
Hard (steel): difficult to magnetise, keeps magnetism — permanent magnets. Soft (iron): easy to magnetise, loses it quickly — electromagnet cores.
ConceptInduced magnetism
A magnet can attract an unmagnetised magnetic material by inducing an opposite pole in the near end.
Field linesDirection and strength
Lines point from N to S (direction of force on a N pole placed there). Closer lines = stronger field.
MethodPlotting a field
Use a plotting compass or iron filings; mark the compass direction repeatedly to trace a full field line.
Electromagnet3 ways to increase strength
More current, more turns of wire, add a soft iron core.
UseWhy electromagnets, not permanent magnets, in cranes
They can be switched on to lift scrap metal and switched off to release it.
SupplementInteracting fields
Attraction/repulsion happens because two magnets' fields combine into a new pattern; magnets move from stronger field to weaker field.
