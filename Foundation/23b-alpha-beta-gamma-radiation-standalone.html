5.2.2 Three types of emission

Alpha, beta and gamma radiation each have their own personality: different charge, different ionising power, and wildly different ability to penetrate matter. Learn to identify each from how it behaves.

W4 A student tests three sources X, Y and Z. Radiation from X is stopped by a sheet of paper; Y is only partially stopped by a few cm of lead; Z is stopped by a few mm of aluminium. What are X, Y and Z?

3Notes

Core

Decay is spontaneous (unaffected by temperature/pressure/chemical state) and random in direction/timing.

TypeNatureChargeIonisingPenetratingStopped by
Alpha (α)Helium nucleus+2StrongestWeakestPaper
Beta (β)Fast electron-1MediumMediumFew mm aluminium
Gamma (γ)EM radiation0WeakestStrongestThick lead
Only α and β change the element (proton number changes); γ just loses energy.
Supplement

Ionising effect depends on charge and speed: slow, highly-charged alpha interacts most (high ionising, low penetration); fast, uncharged gamma interacts least (low ionising, high penetration).

In a field: α and β deflect opposite ways (opposite charge), β deflects more (lighter), γ is undeflected (no charge) — an example of the motor effect (Fleming's left-hand rule).

Full explanations and worked examples: Coursebook Chapter 23.

4Key terms

Pronunciation reference: British Received Pronunciation (RP). /ə/ = the soft "uh" sound in an unstressed syllable — this sound doesn't exist in Bahasa Indonesia.

Ionisationnoun
The process of removing or adding electrons to/from atoms, forming ions.
/ˌaɪənaɪˈzeɪʃən/eye-uh-ny-ZAY-shuhn
🇮🇩ionisasi
"Alpha particles cause strong ionisation along their path."
"Partikel alfa menyebabkan ionisasi kuat di sepanjang lintasannya."
Alpha particle (α)noun
A positively charged particle (2 protons + 2 neutrons, a helium nucleus) emitted from some radioactive nuclei; strongly ionising, weakly penetrating.
/ˈælfə ˈpɑːtɪkəl/AL-fuh PAR-ti-kuhl
🇮🇩partikel alfa
"Alpha particles are stopped by a sheet of paper."
"Partikel alfa dihentikan oleh selembar kertas."
Beta particle (β)noun
A fast-moving electron emitted from the nucleus during radioactive decay; medium ionising, medium penetrating.
/ˈbeɪtə ˈpɑːtɪkəl/BAY-tuh PAR-ti-kuhl
🇮🇩partikel beta
"Beta particles are stopped by a few mm of aluminium."
"Partikel beta dihentikan oleh beberapa mm aluminium."
Gamma ray (γ)noun
High-energy electromagnetic radiation emitted from an unstable nucleus; weakly ionising, strongly penetrating.
/ˈgæmə reɪ/GAM-uh ray
🇮🇩sinar gama
"Gamma rays need thick lead to be significantly absorbed."
"Sinar gama memerlukan timbal tebal untuk diserap secara signifikan."

8Practical check

Aim for 100% before moving on.

P1 A source emits radiation that is not deflected by an electric field, and is only partially stopped by several cm of lead. What type of radiation is this?

P2 A radioactive sample is wrapped in thin (0.1 mm) aluminium foil. A detector 1 cm away in air still records a reading well above background. When a 5 mm sheet of aluminium is placed between source and detector, the reading falls to background level. Which type of radiation is passing through?

P3 A detector records: 480 counts/s in air, 481 counts/s behind thin paper, 479 counts/s behind 2 mm aluminium, and 120 counts/s behind 10 mm lead. Which type(s) of radiation does the source most likely emit?

P4 Which type of radiation consists of a fast-moving electron emitted from the nucleus?

9Flashcards

PropertyNature of α, β, γ
α = helium nucleus (+2). β = fast electron (-1). γ = EM radiation (0 charge).
RankingIonising power
Alpha most ionising > beta > gamma least ionising.
RankingPenetrating power
Gamma most penetrating > beta > alpha least penetrating (opposite order to ionising power).
SupplementField deflection
α and β deflect opposite ways (opposite charge); β deflects more (lighter); γ not deflected (no charge).
