Topic 3 · Waves — Coursebook Chapter 15

3.3 Electromagnetic spectrum

Meet the whole family of electromagnetic waves — from radio waves to gamma rays — and learn why they all behave alike, what makes each region useful, which are hazardous, and how we use them to communicate.

W2 The electromagnetic spectrum is shown in a table from gamma rays to radio waves, with a label missing underneath. Which word is missing?

W3 Which type of electromagnetic wave is used in TV remote controls?

W4 A forgery detector shines electromagnetic radiation onto a banknote, making special ink glow. Which type of radiation is used?

W5 Which sentence about infrared waves is true?

W6 In what way are microwaves different from ultraviolet waves?

3Notes

Core

The electromagnetic spectrum is a family of transverse waves that all travel at the same speed in a vacuum (3.0×108 m/s, ≈ same in air). They differ only in frequency and wavelength (v = fλ, so higher f = shorter λ).

Region (low→high frequency)Typical useHazard of overexposure
Radio wavesBroadcasting, radio astronomy, RFID—
MicrowavesSatellite TV, mobile phones, ovensInternal heating of cells
InfraredRemotes, grills, thermal imaging, optical fibresSkin burns
Visible lightVision, photography—
UltravioletSecurity marking, sterilising waterSkin/eye damage, cancer
X-raysMedical imaging, security scannersCell mutation/damage
Gamma raysSterilising food/equipment, cancer treatmentCell mutation/damage

Satellites communicate by microwaves. Geostationary (~35,000 km): matches Earth's spin, good for TV, has a delay. Low-orbit (~2000 km): no delay, needs many satellites, less data.

Supplement (Extended only)
  • Mobile/wireless internet: microwaves. Bluetooth: radio waves (weakened by walls). Optical fibres: visible light/infrared (glass is transparent to them, high data rate).
  • Analogue signals vary continuously and pick up noise; digital signals (on/off pulses) can be regenerated perfectly — faster, more accurate, longer range.

Full explanations and worked examples: Coursebook Chapter 15.

4Key terms

Pronunciation reference: British Received Pronunciation (RP). /ə/ = the soft "uh" sound in an unstressed syllable — this sound doesn't exist in Bahasa Indonesia.

Electromagnetic spectrumnoun
The full family of transverse waves that includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays.
/ɪˌlektrəʊmæɡˈnetɪk ˈspektrəm/ee-LEK-troh-mag-NET-ik SPEK-truhm
🇮🇩spektrum elektromagnetik
"The electromagnetic spectrum includes light, radio waves and X-rays."
"Spektrum elektromagnetik mencakup cahaya, gelombang radio, dan sinar-X."
Infrarednoun
Electromagnetic radiation with a longer wavelength than visible red light; felt as heat.
/ˌɪnfrəˈred/in-fruh-RED
🇮🇩inframerah
"A remote control uses infrared to send signals to the television."
"Remote kontrol menggunakan sinar inframerah untuk mengirim sinyal ke televisi."
Ultravioletnoun
Electromagnetic radiation with a shorter wavelength than visible violet light.
/ˌʌltrəˈvaɪələt/ul-truh-VY-uh-let
🇮🇩ultraviolet
"Ultraviolet light can be used to sterilise water."
"Cahaya ultraviolet dapat digunakan untuk mensterilkan air."
Wavelengthnoun
The distance from one crest of a wave to the next.
/ˈweɪvleŋθ/WAYV-length
🇮🇩panjang gelombang
"Red light has a longer wavelength than violet light."
"Cahaya merah memiliki panjang gelombang yang lebih panjang daripada cahaya ungu."
Geostationarynoun
Describes a satellite orbit that matches Earth's rotation, so the satellite stays above one point on the equator.
/ˌdʒiːəʊˈsteɪʃənəri/jee-oh-STAY-shuh-nuh-ree
🇮🇩geostasioner
A long word — break it into parts: geo (Earth) + stationary (staying still).
"Geostationary satellites are used for TV broadcasting."
"Satelit geostasioner digunakan untuk siaran TV."
Analogue signalnoun
A signal that varies continuously, matching the original sound or data exactly.
/ˈænəlɒɡ/AN-uh-log
🇮🇩sinyal analog
"A traditional telephone converts sound into an analogue electrical signal."
"Telepon tradisional mengubah suara menjadi sinyal listrik analog."
Digital signalnoun
A signal made of discrete on/off pulses, which can be regenerated cleanly over long distances.
/ˈdɪdʒɪtəl/DIJ-it-uhl
🇮🇩sinyal digital
"Digital signals can travel hundreds of kilometres without distortion."
"Sinyal digital dapat menempuh ratusan kilometer tanpa distorsi."

9Flashcards

Tap a card to flip it.

OrderThe 7 regions
Radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays — increasing frequency, decreasing wavelength.
FactEM wave speed
All electromagnetic waves travel at 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s in a vacuum (and about the same in air).
UseMicrowaves
Satellite TV, mobile phones, microwave ovens.
UseInfrared
Remote controls, grills/toasters, intruder alarms, thermal imaging, optical fibres.
UseUltraviolet
Security marking, detecting forged banknotes, sterilising water.
HazardX-rays & gamma rays
Can mutate or damage body cells — the most hazardous regions.
OrbitGeostationary vs low orbit
Geostationary: far, matches Earth's spin, good for TV, has delay. Low orbit: close, no delay, needs many satellites.
SignalDigital vs analogue
Digital = on/off pulses, can be regenerated cleanly; analogue = continuously varying, picks up noise.
