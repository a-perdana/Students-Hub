4.5.3 Magnetic effect of a current

Every electric current makes its own magnetic field. Explore the field patterns around straight wires and solenoids, how to find their direction, and how this effect powers everyday devices like relays and loudspeakers.

3Notes

Core

Oersted's discovery: any current has a magnetic field around it. A straight wire's field lines are circles around it. Right-hand grip rule: thumb = current direction, curled fingers = field direction.

A solenoid (coil) concentrates the effect: field pattern like a bar magnet's (N pole one end, S the other) — how an electromagnet works. Reveal the pattern with iron filings on a card, or plotting compasses around the wire/coil.

  • Relay: electromagnet lets a small, safe current switch a much larger one (e.g. car ignition → starter motor).
  • Loudspeaker: a coil (on a paper cone) sits in a magnet's field; varying current → varying force (motor effect) → cone moves → sound.
Supplement

Field is stronger closer to the wire/coil and with bigger current; uniform inside a solenoid. Reversing current reverses field direction (swaps solenoid poles).

Full explanations and worked examples: Coursebook Chapter 20.

4Key terms

Pronunciation reference: British Received Pronunciation (RP). /ə/ = the soft "uh" sound in an unstressed syllable — this sound doesn't exist in Bahasa Indonesia.

Solenoidnoun
A coil of wire that produces a magnetic field (like a bar magnet's) when current flows through it.
/ˈsəʊlənɔɪd/SOH-luh-noyd
🇮🇩solenoida
"A solenoid's field looks like a bar magnet's."
"Medan solenoida terlihat seperti medan magnet batang."
Right-hand grip rulenoun
A rule for finding the direction of the magnetic field around a current-carrying wire: grip the wire with the right hand, thumb pointing along the current, fingers show the field direction.
/raɪt hænd ɡrɪp ruːl/ryt-hand grip rool
🇮🇩aturan genggaman tangan kanan
"Use the right-hand grip rule to find the field direction."
"Gunakan aturan genggaman tangan kanan untuk menentukan arah medan."
Relaynoun
An electromagnet-operated switch that lets a small current control a larger one.
/ˈriːleɪ/REE-lay
🇮🇩relai
"A car's ignition uses a relay to start the engine."
"Sistem pengapian mobil menggunakan relai untuk menyalakan mesin."
Loudspeakernoun
A device that uses a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field to move a cone and produce sound.
/ˈlaʊdspiːkə/LOWD-spee-kuh
🇮🇩pengeras suara (loudspeaker)
"The loudspeaker's cone vibrates to create sound."
"Kerucut pengeras suara bergetar untuk menghasilkan suara."
