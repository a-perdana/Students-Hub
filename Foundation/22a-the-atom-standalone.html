5.1.1 The atom

Meet the nuclear model of the atom — a tiny, positive nucleus orbited by electrons — and the astonishing gold-foil experiment that overturned the 'plum pudding' model and proved the nucleus exists.

W2 In the nuclear model of the atom, where is the positive charge located?

W3 Atom X loses an electron to form an ion. Atom Y gains an electron to form an ion. What are the charges on the resulting ions of X and Y?

W4 A nitrogen atom loses an electron. What is it now called?

3Notes

Core

Every atom has a tiny, central, positively charged nucleus, orbited by negatively charged electrons. Equal + and - charge overall → neutral atom.

  • Atom gains an electron → negative ion.
  • Atom loses an electron → positive ion.
Supplement

How we know: Thomson's old "plum pudding" model pictured positive charge spread evenly with electrons embedded in it. Rutherford fired alpha particles (+charged) at thin gold foil: most passed straight through, but a tiny fraction bounced almost straight back.

Conclusion: the nucleus is small, dense, positively charged, holds most of the mass, surrounded by mostly empty space. Alpha particles (charged) were used because they're repelled by the nucleus, revealing it — a neutron wouldn't be.

4Key terms

Nucleusnoun
The tiny, dense, positively charged centre of an atom, containing protons and neutrons.
/ˈnjuːkliəs/NYOO-klee-uhs
🇮🇩inti atom
"The nucleus contains almost all of the atom's mass."
"Inti atom mengandung hampir seluruh massa atom."
Ionnoun
A charged particle formed when an atom gains or loses electrons.
/ˈaɪɒn/EYE-on
🇮🇩ion
"An atom that loses an electron becomes a positive ion."
"Atom yang kehilangan elektron menjadi ion positif."
Alpha particlenoun
A positively charged particle, used by Rutherford to probe the structure of the atom.
/ˈælfə ˈpɑːtɪkəl/AL-fuh PAR-ti-kuhl
🇮🇩partikel alfa
"Alpha particles were fired at gold foil in Rutherford's experiment."
"Partikel alfa ditembakkan ke lempeng emas dalam eksperimen Rutherford."
Plum pudding modelnoun
An early (incorrect) model of the atom as a sphere of positive charge with electrons embedded throughout.
/plʌm ˈpʊdɪŋ ˈmɒdəl/plum PUD-ing MOD-uhl

"The plum pudding model was disproved by Rutherford's experiment."
"Model puding prem dibantah oleh eksperimen Rutherford."

6Theory check

Aim for 100% — if you miss any, press "Try again" and have another go before moving on.

T1 In Rutherford's alpha-scattering experiment, alpha particles were fired at very thin gold foil. Why were alpha particles used rather than neutrons?

T2 A proton and a neutron both pass near a positively charged nucleus. What happens to each, due to the nucleus's charge?

T3 Rutherford's experiment found that most alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil, but a very small fraction bounced almost straight back. What does the fact that MOST particles passed straight through tell us about the atom?

T4 What does the fact that a SMALL FRACTION of alpha particles bounced almost straight back tell us about the nucleus?
