4.5.4 Force on a current-carrying conductor

Discover the motor effect — the force that appears when a current-carrying wire sits in a magnetic field — and learn Fleming's left-hand rule to predict its direction, including for beams of charged particles.

3Notes

Core

A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field feels a force (the wire's field and the magnet's field interact) — the motor effect: current + field = movement. Reverse the current or the field to reverse the force direction.

Supplement

Fleming's left-hand rule (force, field, current all at right angles):

  • First finger → Field
  • SeCond finger → Current
  • ThuMb → force / Motion

A magnetic field also deflects charged-particle beams (e.g. electrons) the same way — remember conventional current is opposite to electron flow direction. Used in particle accelerators (e.g. CERN) to focus/steer beams.

Full explanations and worked examples: Coursebook Chapter 20.

4Key terms

Pronunciation reference: British Received Pronunciation (RP). /ə/ = the soft "uh" sound in an unstressed syllable — this sound doesn't exist in Bahasa Indonesia.

Motor effectnoun
The force produced on a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field.
/ˈməʊtər ɪˈfekt/MOH-tuh ee-FEKT
🇮🇩efek motor
"The motor effect makes a wire move when current flows in a magnetic field."
"Efek motor membuat kawat bergerak saat arus mengalir dalam medan magnet."
Fleming's left-hand rulenoun
A rule using the left hand's thumb and first two fingers to find the relative directions of field, current and force.
/ˈflemɪŋz left hænd ruːl/FLEM-ingz left-hand rool

"Use Fleming's left-hand rule to predict the direction of the force."
"Gunakan kaidah tangan kiri Fleming untuk memprediksi arah gaya."
Charged particle beamnoun
A stream of moving charged particles (like electrons or ions) that can be deflected by magnetic fields.

6Theory check

Aim for 100% — if you miss any, press "Try again" and have another go before moving on.

T1 A beam of positively charged alpha particles moves through a magnetic field pointing out of the page (towards you). Using Fleming's left-hand rule (First finger = field, seCond finger = current in the direction the positive particles move, thuMb = force), which direction is the force on the beam if the field points out of the page and the particles move to the right?

T2 A current-carrying wire is placed between the poles of two magnets and experiences an upward force. The magnetic field between the poles points from left to right. Using Fleming's left-hand rule, which pole is on the left and which is on the right?

T3 An electron travels through a uniform magnetic field that points into the page. Since electrons are negatively charged, in which direction does the EQUIVALENT conventional current point, if the electron itself moves from right to left?

T4 Which statement correctly distinguishes the motor effect from the generator effect (met in the next chapter)?
